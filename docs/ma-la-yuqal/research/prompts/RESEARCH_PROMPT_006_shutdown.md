# أمر بحث 006 — جاهز للصق بأداة البحث
الأداة: أي مساعد بحث (Deep Research أو غيره). انسخ الكتلة بالأسفل كما هي. بعدها ابعتلي النتيجة وبدقق عليها بنفس منهج المختبر.

```
You are a careful research assistant for an Arabic (Jordanian) podcast called «ما لا يُقال» ("What Is Left Unsaid"). The host wants a research brief for a solo episode about this experience:

"A person gets through a hard period by switching off part of their feelings. It works: they keep going. Later they notice the cost. They no longer reach out when someone disappears for a week. They leave messages from people unopened. They feel guilty about it. People around them read it as coldness or as 'dead feelings'. They also get tired of explaining and justifying themselves and of being analysed by others."

GOAL
Find out what is known, what is not known, and how people describe this in everyday Jordanian/Levantine Arabic.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS
1. What does peer-reviewed research say about deliberately dampening or suppressing emotions to get through a demanding period? Cover expressive suppression, emotional avoidance, emotional numbing or blunting, protective detachment and emotional distancing. Separate short-term from long-term findings. State limits: sample, correlation vs causation, culture.
2. What is known about emotional flexibility, meaning people who can both dampen and express emotion as the situation requires (for example work by Bonanno and colleagues)? Verify every citation before using it.
3. How do researchers distinguish everyday protective numbing from depression, burnout, empathy or compassion fatigue, and trauma responses? We do not need a diagnosis. We need accurate wording and a clear statement of when professional help is suggested.
4. What does research say about judging a person's character from a single moment (fundamental attribution error, negativity bias, thin-slice judgments)? What does it say about the fatigue of having to explain oneself?
5. Arab and Jordanian context: peer-reviewed or reputable work on emotional restraint, patience («الصبر»), «خليها بالقلب», and gender and emotional expression in Jordan and the Levant.
6. Language: collect how people say this in Jordanian/Levantine Arabic (for example «طفّيت», «ما عاد يفرق معي», «صرت حجر»). For each: the expression, the situation, and a confidence level. Mark anything you cannot verify.

OUTPUT FORMAT (write in clear Arabic)
- A table of sources: title, authors, year, journal or organization, link, type (peer-reviewed, review, report, news), what it shows, limits, and what you actually read (full text, abstract, or secondary summary).
- Tag every claim: A fact, B participant account, C observation, D interpretation, E editorial idea, F unknown.
- Three sections: "What we can say", "What we cannot say", "Open questions".
- 8–12 everyday expressions with confidence levels.
- Counter-examples: when switching off is helpful, and when it is costly.

RULES
Do not diagnose the host or anyone.
Do not claim that suppressing emotions causes any illness.
Do not invent studies, authors, numbers, quotes or links.
Do not cite a source you did not open. If you saw only an abstract or a secondary summary, say so.
Do not use social media posts as evidence of how Jordanians feel.
Do not quote usernames or post titles from Reddit or other forums.
Do not give advice or self-help steps.
Do not describe the episode as therapy.
Do not generalise from one country, culture, gender or age group to all Arabs.
Do not label regions of Jordan.
Never present a statistic without its source, year and definition.
```

1. لصقها هون.
2. تدقيقها: كل رابط ورقم ووسم.
3. تحديث السجلات (S, Q, القرارات).
4. مدخل الأطلس وهيكل الحلقة.
